Google, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn, newsletters, paid ads. With so many options, it's easy to spread yourself across all of them and succeed at none. Good promotion doesn't start with "where is everyone?" It starts with "where do our clients look for us, and what can we realistically keep up over time?"

## Three questions before you pick a channel

**Where is your client when they need you?** Someone who needs an emergency plumber is on Google. Someone looking for wedding design ideas is on Instagram. Someone choosing an accountant for their company asks colleagues on LinkedIn.

**What can you produce consistently?** A channel that needs three videos a week won't work if you have no time to film. One channel you keep alive beats three you abandoned.

**How will you know it's working?** Decide up front what success means: enquiries, calls, sign-ups. Likes on their own are not a result.

## Your website is the hub

Whatever channel you choose, it ends up in the same place: your website. A post, an ad or a search result brings a visitor, and the site has to finish the job. If it's slow, unclear or doesn't inspire trust, every dollar you put into promotion loses part of its value. That's why it pays to strengthen your site before you increase any budget.

## Start small and measure

Pick one or two channels, give them a few months, and track how many enquiries each one brings. What works gets more time and budget. What doesn't, after a fair try, you can drop without guilt.
