Most business owners don't lose their time on the big jobs. It goes on the small things that come back every day: answering the same question again, copying a client's details from a message into a spreadsheet, digging through old emails to remember what was agreed. Each one takes a few minutes, and together they can eat up hours every week.

## Step one: write down what keeps repeating

Before you look at tools, spend one week noting every task you did more than twice. By the end of the week you'll have a short list of things worth simplifying. Usually it's answering common questions, booking meetings, collecting enquiries and following up with clients who went quiet.

## Start with your website

Your website is the first place a client meets you, so that's where the savings start. A good FAQ page answers a lot of questions before anyone picks up the phone. A clear contact form gathers every enquiry in one place, and a WhatsApp button gives people a fast way to reach you without hunting for a number.

## Don't overdo it

Automation should save you time, not hand you a new project. If a tool takes more time to set up and maintain than it saves, it isn't right for a small business. Start with one thing, give it a few weeks, and only then add the next.
