Plenty of businesses get traffic to their website and very few enquiries. The problem usually isn't how many people visit, it's what happens once they arrive. A lead is someone who has decided you're worth a message. This guide is about one thing: helping them decide, and making it easy to get in touch.

## A good lead starts with a clear offer

If a visitor can't tell within a few seconds what you do and who it's for, they leave. The first headline on your site should say what you offer, who it's for and what they get out of it. "Mortgage advice for first-time buyers, without leaving home" works far better than "advanced financial solutions". The sharper the offer, the better the enquiries you receive.

## A shorter form means more enquiries

Every extra field on a form is another reason to give up. For most businesses, a name, a phone number or email, and one line for "how can we help" is plenty. Everything else can wait for the conversation. If you need to filter, one smart question, like the type of service, beats five required fields.

## Trust is built from details

People don't reach out to a business they aren't sure is real. A clear name and location, a photo of the person behind the business, an explanation of how the process works, and examples of past work all lower the barrier to getting in touch.

## How fast you reply

Leads go cold quickly. Someone who contacted three businesses will usually go with whoever replied first. Make sure every enquiry lands somewhere you actually look, and get into the habit of replying the same day.
